AN ORIGINAL HOMAGE · NOT A TRANSCRIPT OF THE SKETCH

Listen between the words.

?

The question

Does the opening sound curious? Listen for question intonation, not just a pause.

,

The breath

A brief pause should group ideas naturally without chopping up the sentence.

; however,

The turn

A longer transition invites a change of emphasis. Does the contrast come through?

!

The enthusiasm

A little energy, a little flourish. Does the voice sell the wonderfully absurd promise?

.

The landing

Listen for a completed thought, a clean stop, and room for the next sentence.

Aa

The technobabble

Invented terms test fluency and consistency. There is no single correct pronunciation to score.

Punctuation is a cue, not a guaranteed performance control. This is an audition, not a scientific ranking: listen for pronunciation, rhythm, warmth, and the voice that fits your work.

1944→1970s

AN ENGINEERING JOKE WITH LEGS

About that turbo-encabulator…

In 1944, John Hellins Quick described an imaginary machine in an engineering students' journal. Its wonderfully confident jargon sounded technical, but explained absolutely nothing. Engineers have been in on the joke ever since.

In the late 1970s, industrial-film actor Bud Haggart brought it to the screen. After a GMC Trucks shoot, he and director Dave Rondot filmed the spoof with the straight-faced delivery that made it a classic. Our new audition passage borrows that spirit, not the original script.

02 / THE RECORDING

Made on a Mac Studio. No cloud.

Every recording was generated on our own hardware using Kokoro and MLX-Audio, with the model already downloaded and speech synthesis running offline. Our script stayed in our custody throughout recording: no text was sent to an external speech service, and no cloud API generated the audio.

FFmpeg prepared the finished recordings for the web. All presets use the same English passage at 1.0× synthesis speed; non-English presets use English phonemization for this comparison.
